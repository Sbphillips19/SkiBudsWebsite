To read a ski resort snow forecast, check the accumulation period, expected snowfall timing, forecast elevation, wind, and update time. Then compare the outlook with the resort's current reports. The biggest snow total does not tell you everything about the day you are planning.

This guide explains how to interpret forecast information for a resort trip. Check the resort's official updates for operating decisions and the relevant public agencies for road and avalanche information.

## Check which period the snow total covers

A 24-hour total, a 48-hour total, and a weekly total describe different periods. Before comparing two forecasts, make sure you know when each period starts and ends. A weekly total may include snow expected well after the day you plan to ride.

In the SkiBuds rebuild, the seven-day snow view covers seven resort-local calendar days, including the current day. It is different from promising a rolling 168-hour total starting at the moment you open the app. Our [weather feature preview](/weather/) explains the available views.

## Look at when the snow is expected

Use the hourly forecast to see whether snowfall is expected overnight, during the morning, or later in the day. Two days with similar totals can have different timing relative to your arrival.

For example, a forecast of new snow mainly after the lifts close describes a different plan from one with snow expected before first chair. Treat that timing as an outlook to revisit, rather than a fixed appointment with the weather.

## Check the location and elevation

Read the location and elevation behind a forecast. A town forecast, a resort base forecast, and a mountain forecast may describe different reference points.

The [National Weather Service's mountain forecast guide](https://www.weather.gov/media/wrh/avalanche/AVG_Guide.pdf) distinguishes snow level from the accumulation forecast and reports winds at specified elevations. Look at those labels before comparing numbers across locations.

SkiBuds includes base, mid-mountain, and summit forecast views in the rebuild. The detail depends on the source: some providers do not supply a distinct forecast for each selected elevation. A selected summit view is not a measurement taken at the summit.

## Read wind alongside snowfall

Look at forecast wind and gusts along with snow and temperature. Note the time and elevation each wind forecast describes. Those details help you understand the outlook for the part of the mountain you want to visit.

Forecast wind is separate from a confirmed lift status. Read the resort's own operating updates to find out what is open, on hold, or closed.

## Separate new snow from snowpack depth

Forecast snowfall is an estimate of new snow over a stated period. Observed snow depth describes the snowpack at a measurement location. They answer different questions and should not be treated as interchangeable totals.

The [USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service's SNOTEL network](https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/data-and-reports/snow-and-water-interactive-map) provides station data. When SkiBuds shows nearby SNOTEL observations, those are station readings or station context; they are not a resort-wide fresh-snow measurement.

## Check freshness and uncertainty

Look at when the forecast was updated and whether the app is displaying saved or stale data. Recheck as your trip approaches, especially if the plan was made several days earlier.

The [National Weather Service's probabilistic snowfall products](https://www.weather.gov/prob-snow) show ranges and probabilities to communicate uncertainty. A single forecast number should be read with that uncertainty in mind.

SkiBuds forecast confidence reflects context such as available source agreement. It is not a calibrated probability that a particular snowfall amount will occur. Sources can agree and the weather can still develop differently.

## Turn the forecast into a shared plan

Before you [meet a new riding buddy](/find-ski-buddies/) or [confirm a ski carpool](/guides/ski-carpool-planning/), agree on when you will check the outlook again. Put the mountain, arrival time, and any change to the plan in the same conversation.

A suggested riding window can help you choose what to investigate next. It does not establish avalanche safety, open terrain, operating lifts, or suitable road conditions. Use the relevant official information for those decisions.
