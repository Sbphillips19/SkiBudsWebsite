To find ski buddies, start with a local ski club, a group around your home resort, or a rider discovery app. Share the mountain, date, terrain, and pace you have in mind, then suggest a specific meeting point. A clear plan gives people something they can say yes to.

You do not need a large crew. One person with a compatible schedule can turn “none of my friends can go” into a day you can plan together.

## Start with the day you actually want

Before searching for people to ski or snowboard with, decide what kind of day you are looking for. “Anyone want to ride?” makes other people do the planning. “Blue groomers on Saturday morning, with a break around noon” gives them a useful picture.

Write down the resort and date, your usual terrain, the pace you like, and whether you want a full day or just a few laps. Include whether you already have transport and lift access. If you are learning, say that you want an easy pace; if you want to practice a specific skill, explain it.

Ability labels can mean different things to different riders. Talking about actual runs and preferences is more useful than expecting a label to settle every question.

## Where to look for ski and snowboard partners

### Local ski clubs

Look for clubs near where you live or the mountain you visit. A club can give you a recurring way to meet people, especially if your schedule lines up with its trips or social events. Check the group's current membership rules, trip calendar, and pace before joining.

SkiBuds' [partners page](/affiliates/) includes SnowPals, a San Francisco Bay Area snow-sports club with a focus on Lake Tahoe trips. It is one starting point for that region; use the same approach to search for clubs near your own mountain.

### Resort and city groups

Try the communities around both ends of your trip: the place you leave from and the mountain you want to ride. A city group may be more useful for sharing the drive, while a resort group may help you find someone who is already nearby.

Read the group rules, look at recent activity, and use any designated buddy or carpool thread. Share your general area in a public post and save exact pickup details for a private conversation after you decide who to meet.

### Lessons, events, and existing friends

A group lesson or resort social event gives you a chance to meet people with something in common. Ask friends whether they know another skier or snowboarder who goes on similar days. An introduction can make a first meetup easier to arrange.

### Rider discovery apps

An app can make profiles, riding interests, and conversations easier to organize. Check whether it is available where you ride and whether its discovery preferences match your needs.

## Write a meetup post people can answer

Use a short, specific message:

> “Looking for a ski or snowboard buddy for Saturday at [resort]. I enjoy blue groomers at a relaxed pace and plan to ride from about 9 to 1. Happy to meet by the main base map and start with a couple of laps. Anyone have a similar plan?”

Replace the terrain and timing with your own preferences. If you also need transport, explain that separately instead of assuming a riding partner can offer a seat.

## Make the first meetup manageable

Agree on a public meeting point and a time. Exchange enough contact information to handle a delay, and confirm the day before you go. Start with a few runs you both want to ride so you can see whether your pace works together.

Discuss breaks and where to regroup if you separate. Leave room for either person to finish early. A good first day does not depend on riding every run together.

If you are [planning a carpool](/guides/ski-carpool-planning/), agree on the return time before leaving home. That decision affects both the driver and everyone who needs a lift back.

## Make the next day easier to arrange

After a good meetup, exchange plans for another date. A small group conversation with a mountain and a day in mind is easier to keep moving than a vague promise to ride sometime.

Before the next trip, [check the snow forecast](/guides/how-to-read-snow-forecasts/) together and confirm the plan again. Building a ski crew is often a sequence of small, repeatable days rather than one perfect introduction.
