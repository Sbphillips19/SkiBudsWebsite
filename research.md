# Research: SkiBuds Keyword & Competitor Landscape (pre-launch SEO targets)

## Summary

Search demand clusters around three intents: (1) finding people to ride with ("ski buddy", "find ski partners"), (2) logistics ("ski carpool [resort]", "rideshare to [resort]"), and (3) resort-community/conditions queries dominated by resort sites, Facebook groups, and Reddit. The competitor field is fragmented — no dominant dedicated ski-partner app has survived at scale — so SkiBuds' unique-value pages are those matching _people + trip logistics per resort_, not generic "ski conditions" or evergreen content where resorts/OnTheSnow/Reddit already win.

**Methodology caveat:** No live web-search tool was available in this runtime; findings below are based on training knowledge of SERP behavior, established SEO patterns for this niche, and known competitor landscape. Treat volume figures as directional ranges, not tool exports. Recommend validating with Ahrefs/Semrush/Google Keyword Planner before committing the content calendar.

## Findings

### Demand & intent

1. **"Ski buddy / find ski partners" is a real but modest-volume, high-intent head term.** Queries like "ski buddy app", "find someone to ski with", "snowboard partner app" show consistent long-tail demand; intent is transactional (user wants a solution now), which fits a pre-launch app landing page perfectly. Volume is low (est. low hundreds/mo combined US) but conversion-relevant.
2. **Ski carpool queries are resort-modified and seasonal.** Patterns like "ski carpool [city] to [resort]", "rideshare to Whistler", "carpool to Breckenridge from Denver" spike Nov–Apr. These are underserved: Craigslist rideshares are dead-ish, Facebook groups are unindexed, and existing options are scattered. This is SkiBuds' strongest defensible cluster.
3. **Resort community searches are mostly captured by Facebook groups and Reddit.** e.g., "Whistler ski buddies group", "Vail locals Facebook", r/Whistler, r/skiing threads asking "how do I find people to ride with solo" recur every season. Google often surfaces Reddit directly — meaning a thin programmatic "[Resort] community page" will lose; only pages with genuinely useful local utility (active partner feed, carpool board, conditions summary) can compete.
4. **Solo-rider pain point is a documented, recurring topic.** Reddit/forum threads on "skiing alone", "how to meet skiers" get steady engagement — strong evidence of unmet demand and good raw material for an authoritative pillar page.

### Competitor landscape

5. **No incumbent rider-matching app dominates.** Past attempts (e.g., Ski Buddy-type apps, SnoParty, SkiGala-style socials, SnowPals for Tahoe/Whistler) are small, regional, stale, or community-run rather than products. Facebook Groups remain the de facto standard per-resort. This fragmentation is SkiBuds' opportunity — and also means no competitor earns these keywords well.
6. **SnowPals (snowpals.com)** — member-driven buddy/carpool listings focused on Whistler & Tahoe; proves the model works regionally but is dated UX and not app-based. Direct model to study, weak SEO threat.
7. **Facebook Groups + Reddit + Mountain Collective/Epic/Ikon community channels** own "community" intent indirectly. Unindexable, so they leave an indexable gap — but Google increasingly shows Reddit threads, so content must add utility beyond a forum answer.
8. **Conditions/forecast space is saturated** (OpenSnow, OnTheSnow, resort sites, NOAA). Do NOT build conditions-led SEO pages; use conditions only as enrichment on partner/carpool pages.

### Priority rationale

- Build 1, 4, 5 first (evergreen, product-aligned, linkable).
- Launch resort pages only for markets with confirmed user density; expand programmatically **only** once live-data embeds exist.
- Skip conditions-only and generic "[resort] ski guide" pages — unwinnable against OpenSnow/resort sites.
